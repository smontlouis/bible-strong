# Legal framework and Audible / audiobook distribution route for audio Bibles (as of November 2026)

> Method note. Research done 24 Nov 2026 through web search only: direct page fetches of `help.acx.com`, `support.spotify.com` and `www.faithcomesbyhearing.com` were blocked by the session's network settings, so findings from those sites come from search-result extracts of the pages, not a full read. Items marked **[unverified]** rest on secondary sources or on background knowledge not re-checked against a primary source in this session. This is informational, not legal advice; points flagged **[lawyer]** should be confirmed by a French IP lawyer.

## 1. Rights layers: translation text vs. sound recording (France/EU and US); is a 1950s recording such as Scourby's KJV still protected?

### Takeaway
An audio Bible stacks three independent rights: copyright in the translation text, the producer's right in the recording, and the narrator's performer right; a public-domain text does not make a recording free. A 1950s recording is very probably out of producer/performer protection in the EU (the 70-year extension only covered recordings still protected on 1 Nov 2013) but is still federally protected in the US until the 2060s, and the Scourby recordings are actively enforced by Litchfield Associates.

### Cited Findings
- EU Directive 2011/77/EU raised the term for performers and phonogram producers from 50 to 70 years. — [CMS Law](https://cms.law/en/fra/legal-updates/la-france-allonge-la-duree-de-protection-des-droits-voisins-dans-le-secteur-de-la-musique); directive text: [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/77/oj/eng)
- The extended term applies only to fixations and phonograms for which the performer and producer were still protected on 1 November 2013, and to later ones. — [Directive 2011/77/EU, art. 1 (legislation.gov.uk copy)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2011/77/article/1)
- France transposed this by rewriting article L211-4 CPI (previously 50 years from the performance or fixation), with start-date rules differing by category of right holder, and added L212-3-1 to L212-3-4 as the performers' counterpart. — [Sénat report l14-172](https://www.senat.fr/rap/l14-172/l14-1726.html); article text: [Legifrance L211-4](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/id/LEGIARTI000006279031/2006-08-03) (link returned by search points to a historical version; current wording not re-read)
- US: Title II of the Music Modernization Act (Classics Protection and Access Act) brings pre-1972 sound recordings partially into the federal system. — [US Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/music-modernization/pre1972-soundrecordings/)
- US terms under 17 USC 1401: recordings first published 1947–1956 are protected for 95 years plus 15 additional years (110 years from publication); recordings from 1957 to 14 Feb 1972 are protected until 15 Feb 2067. — [17 USC 1401 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1401); [CRS Legal Sidebar LSB10181](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB10181.html)
- One university table gives 115 years for 1947–1956 recordings; this conflicts with the statute summary and CRS (110 years). — [OSU Libraries](https://library.osu.edu/site/publicdomain/2020/07/27/when-does-music-enter-the-public-domain-in-the-united-states); contradicted by [CRS LSB10181](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB10181.html)
- Scourby recorded the KJV in the 1950s for the American Foundation for the Blind. — [Wikipedia, Audio Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_Bible); [AFB](https://afb.org/node/16284)
- Current Scourby listings carry Litchfield Associates notices dated 1991, 2009 and 2019; some Audible listings read "Public Domain (P)2019 Litchfield Associates" (text public domain, recording claimed). — [Audible, Book of Psalms KJV, Scourby Bible Media](https://audible.com/pd/Book-of-Psalms-King-James-Bible-Audiobook/B08DJ8R4H3)
- Litchfield Associates Ltd., owner of the Scourby KJV audio, sued the LDS Church in 2014 for copyright infringement and breach of contract over use of the recordings in apps; the case settled in March 2015. — [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/hunterschwarz/mormon-church-sued-for-copyright-infringement-over-bible-rec); [Law360](https://law360.com/amp/articles/547260)

### Inferences
- EU/France: a recording first published in, say, 1953–1956 had a 50-year producer term that ran out around 2004–2007, before the 1 Nov 2013 cut-off, so it was not revived. Recordings published from 1963 onward got 70 years. This is a reading of the directive, not a statement found in a source **[lawyer]**.
- US: a recording first published in 1953 would be protected through 2063 (1953 + 110); so the Scourby KJV cannot be streamed to US users without a licence even if the EU producer right has lapsed.
- Practical consequence for an app with French and English/US users: territory-by-territory clearance, or a licence from the party claiming the recording. The Litchfield litigation shows that an app is exactly the kind of use that gets enforced.
- Later remasters or re-recordings (the 1991/2009/2019 (P) lines) may carry their own, later term; which master a given file comes from matters.
- Performer moral rights in France (right to name and respect of the performance) are not time-limited in the same way as economic rights **[unverified — background knowledge, lawyer]**.

### Gaps
- Current in-force wording of L211-4 CPI not read directly (Legifrance link returned was a historical version).
- Exact first-publication year(s) of the Scourby KJV, whether there are several distinct Scourby recordings, and the chain of title AFB → Alexander Scourby Bible Recordings → Litchfield: not established.
- Whether French law would treat a US 1950s recording as protected at all (rule of the shorter term / Rome Convention points of attachment): not researched **[lawyer]**.

## 2. Public-domain status of the translations (France/EU and US) and the pitfalls of revised editions

### Takeaway
Segond 1910, the KJV outside the UK, WEB and BSB are confirmed public domain by sources found; the older French versions (Martin, Darby, Ostervald 1877, Crampon 1923, Rabbinat) are public domain by date arithmetic but I found no authoritative rights statement for them. Modern revisions (Segond 21, NEG 1979, and possibly Ostervald 1996) must be treated as separately protected or uncertain.

### Cited Findings
- Louis Segond 1910: stated to be public domain and not copyrighted. — [eBible.org](https://ebible.org/fraLSG/copyright.htm)
- Segond 21 is not public domain: © 2007 Société Biblique de Genève. — [BibleServer](https://www.bibleserver.com/fr/verse/Luc1%3A9)
- KJV: rights are vested in the Crown under royal letters patent and administered in the UK by Cambridge University Press as the Crown's patentee; liturgical use up to 500 verses or less than a full book needs no application, other uses need written permission. — [Cambridge copyright notice](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805211/63347/copyright/9780521163347_copyright_info.pdf)
- Cambridge's Bibles permissions form asks for number of verses, purpose, territory and format, and asks non-UK publishers to estimate UK distribution. — [Cambridge Bibles Request Form](https://www.cambridge.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Bibles%20Request%20Form%20-%20Permissions.pdf)
- The letters patent have no expiry date and have no effect outside the UK, where the KJV is in the public domain; CUP, OUP and Collins hold the UK printing privilege. — [eBible.org, KJV Cambridge Paragraph Bible](https://ebible.org/engkjvcpb/copyright.htm) (third-party statement, not Cambridge's own)
- World English Bible: text dedicated to the public domain; "World English Bible" is a trademark of eBible.org and may not be used for altered text; an MP3 edition exists with the same notice. — [eBible.org WEB copyright page](https://ebible.org/engwebp/copyright.htm); [eBible.org WEB mp3 notice](https://ebible.org/engwebp/mp3/copr.htm)
- Berean Standard Bible: text dedicated to the public domain as of 30 April 2023; audio and app use explicitly encouraged; derivative texts that differ should not use the Berean name. — [berean.bible terms](https://berean.bible/terms.htm)
- A BSB audio narration is also stated to be dedicated to the public domain. — [YouVersion BSB audio listing](https://bible.com/audio-bible-app-versions/3034)
- Ostervald: two sites label the text (including the "1744 rev. 1996" edition) public domain without naming a rights holder; the 1996 revision is described as a revision of the Froussard 1881 edition by missionary C. H. Boughman, distributed by Bearing Precious Seed. — [Bible Study Tools](https://www.biblestudytools.com/ost/); [Textus Receptus wiki](https://textus-receptus.com/wiki/French_Portal) (weak secondary sources)
- Crampon is dated 1923 by some listings and 1904 by others. — [bible.audio version list](https://www.bible.audio/affichage-multi-bible.php?version=CRA)

### Inferences
- France/EU rule is 70 years after the author's death (L123-1 CPI); US rule is that works published before 1931 are public domain in 2026. Both stated from background knowledge, not re-sourced **[unverified]**. Applying them: Martin (1744), Darby French (1885, Darby d. 1882), Ostervald 1877 revision, Segond (d. 1885) and Zadoc Kahn (d. 1905, Rabbinat Bible 1899–1906) are far outside both terms; ASV 1901 is US public domain by date. Death dates are from memory **[unverified]**.
- Crampon 1923 is the fragile case in France: Crampon died in 1894 but the 1923 edition was revised by others; if any identified reviser died after 1955, French protection could persist; if it is a collective/anonymous work, 70 years from publication has passed. In the US it is public domain by publication date **[lawyer]**.
- "Segond 1910" files circulating online are not all the 1910 text: Segond "révisée" (Colombe 1978), NEG 1979, Segond 21 (2007) and "Segond avec Strong" data sets carry later rights. The narration script should be taken from a verified 1910 source.
- The 1996 Ostervald revision is a derivative of a public-domain text; whether the revisers claim copyright is not established. Until a rights statement is obtained, treat it as protected or use the 1877 text.
- For the UK, a self-produced KJV recording sold on Audible UK technically touches the Crown patent; whether the patent reaches audio (it concerns printing) is unclear **[lawyer]**.
- Added matter is separately protectable even on public-domain texts: section headings, notes, cross-references, modernised spelling, and (in the EU) critical/scientific editions; read only the bare text.

### Gaps
- No authoritative rights statement found for Darby (FR), Martin, Ostervald 1877/1996, Crampon 1923, Bible du Rabbinat, NEG 1979 or ASV in this session; statuses above are by reasoning.
- Rights holder of NEG 1979 (believed Société Biblique de Genève) not confirmed by a source.
- Cambridge's own position on audio recordings of the KJV, inside or outside the UK, not found.

## 3. Audible: ACX eligibility, public-domain and AI rules, royalties, specs — and alternative routes from France

### Takeaway
ACX is closed to French residents and does not accept bare public-domain texts or third-party AI narration, so ACX is not a workable route for a France-based developer's own recording of Segond 1910 or the KJV. Reaching Audible from France means going through an aggregator or a direct publisher deal; Voices by INaudio (ex-Findaway) accepts original recordings of worldwide-public-domain titles, but whether those are passed on to Audible is not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- ACX is open to residents of the US, Canada, UK and Ireland with valid tax information; all payees must supply taxpayer identification in the ACX tax profile. — [ACX help, tax forms](https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-tax-forms)
- ACX: public-domain books "are not permitted to be posted as a project on ACX", with one exception requiring that you created your own version by adding your own original content. — [ACX help, how audiobook rights work](https://help.acx.com/s/article/how-audiobook-rights-work)
- ACX submission requirements (reported as revised 15 April 2026) require human narration unless otherwise authorised and list unauthorised text-to-speech, AI and automated recordings as prohibited. — [ACX audio submission requirements](https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-audio-submission-requirements) (page not readable in this session; wording as reported by [Inkfluence AI](https://www.inkfluenceai.com/blog/ai-narration-audible-acx-policy-2026), a vendor blog)
- Audible's own AI routes are separate: "Virtual Voice" through KDP, labelled as such to listeners, and ACX "Voice Replica", an AI copy of a narrator's own voice built from recordings the narrator submits. — [ACX Voice Replica help](https://help.acx.com/s/article/voice-replica-help); [Jane Friedman](https://janefriedman.com/acx-audible-rolls-out-new-ai-narration-program/)
- ACX royalties: new model from 26 May 2026 for new titles and users — 50% exclusive, 30% non-exclusive; legacy 40% / 25% continues to year-end; from 1 Jan 2027 Audible begins to stop distributing titles not enrolled by 31 Dec 2026. — [ACX help, Audible's new royalty model](https://help.acx.com/s/article/audible-s-new-royalty-model); [ACX blog](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/audible-new-royalty-model)
- ACX's own pages conflict on whether the new rates cover à-la-carte sales or only membership listening. — [ACX help](https://help.acx.com/s/article/audible-s-new-royalty-model); contradicted by [ACX blog](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/audible-new-royalty-model)
- ACX agreements (pay-for-production exclusive and non-exclusive, licence and distribution agreement) were revised 26 May 2026. — [ACX legal contracts](https://help.acx.com/s/article/legal-contracts)
- ACX audio specs exist for encoding, peak and RMS levels and are checked by ACX "Audio Lab" on seven metrics. — [ACX audio submission requirements](https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-audio-submission-requirements); [ACX blog, Audio Lab](https://www.acx.com/mp/blog/sound-check-audio-lab-launches-on-acx)
- Findaway Voices was renamed Voices by INaudio on 1 August 2025; Spotify for Authors is now a separate product for Spotify-only distribution, from which a title can be sent on to Voices by INaudio for other retailers. — [Spotify support](https://support.spotify.com/ar/authors/article/spotify-for-authors-findaway-voices); [Reedsy review](https://reedsy.com/blog/voices-by-inaudio-review/)
- Voices/Findaway public-domain policy: you may submit your own original recording of a public-domain title that meets quality guidelines, not someone else's performance; the title must be public domain worldwide because all titles need worldwide rights; "Public Domain" is not a valid copyright line; no production or casting support for public-domain titles. — [Spotify/Findaway public domain policy](https://support.spotify.com/bs/findawayvoices/article/public-domain-content); [Voices by INaudio copy](https://voicessupport.inaudio.com/en/articles/3222592)
- Voices by INaudio keeps 20% and pays 80% of net receipts; partner rates run 40–50% of list price (à la carte), 30–40% (subscription), 25–45% of receipts (revenue share); the full partner list is shown inside the account. — [Spotify/Findaway royalty rates](https://support.spotify.com/fj/findawayvoices/article/royalty-rates); [Reedsy](https://reedsy.com/blog/voices-by-inaudio-review/)
- Spotify accepts ElevenLabs-narrated audiobooks, distributed via Findaway Voices to Spotify and "select other" retailers and flagged as digitally narrated in metadata. — [Spotify newsroom, 20 Feb 2025](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-02-20/spotify-opens-up-support-for-elevenlabs-audiobook-content/); [ElevenLabs blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/spotify-is-now-accepting-audiobooks-narrated-by-elevenlabs)
- Apple Books has its own digital-narration programme. — [Apple Books partner support](https://itunespartner.apple.com/books/support/46-digital-narration)
- Audio Publishers Association guidelines (Oct 2024): AI narration is signalled to retailers through title metadata. — [APA](https://www.audiopub.org/naming-guidelines-for-ai-narrated-audiobooks)
- Libro.fm states it will never knowingly promote AI-narrated audiobooks. — [Libro.fm](https://blog.libro.fm/an-update-on-libro-fms-response-to-ai-narration/)
- France: Audible France reports taking in 2,500–3,000 French-language titles a year; Audiolib produces and distributes for major houses; Prose is a French audiobook platform for independent bookshops that excludes Audible-produced titles. — [Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/une-plateforme-de-livres-audio-pour-les-librairies-independantes); [ActuaLitté](https://actualitte.com/article/94277/distribution/audible-fait-de-l-evangelisation-avec-ses-livres-audio)

### Inferences
- A French sole developer cannot open an ACX account (residency), and even an eligible account could not post a plain Segond 1910 or KJV reading (public-domain rule) nor an ElevenLabs reading (AI rule). Three independent blockers.
- The many public-domain Bibles already on Audible (see section 4) therefore arrive through publisher/aggregator accounts with direct Audible contracts, not through ACX self-service.
- The "worldwide public domain" condition at Voices by INaudio fits Segond 1910, Martin, Darby, WEB, BSB; it is awkward for the KJV (UK Crown patent) and for Crampon 1923 if its French status is uncertain.
- ACX's commonly quoted technical targets — MP3 192 kbps CBR 44.1 kHz, RMS −23 to −18 dB, peaks ≤ −3 dB, noise floor ≤ −60 dB, files ≤ 120 min, 0.5–1 s head and 1–5 s tail room tone, opening/closing credits and a retail sample — are from background knowledge **[unverified]**; mastering to them is still sensible since aggregators use near-identical specs.

### Gaps
- Whether Voices by INaudio (or any aggregator) currently delivers to Audible, and whether Audible takes public-domain or AI titles through it: not confirmed; the partner list is only visible in-account.
- Nothing found on Bible-specific rules at ACX or Audible.
- No usable information found on Author's Republic, Bookwire, Zebralution, Kontakt Audio, Kobo Writing Life, Storytel, BookBeat, Nextory or Lizzie regarding public-domain/Bible/AI acceptance or Audible delivery. These need direct enquiry.
- Whether the Findaway/Spotify 20% fee waiver on Spotify sales still applies after the 2025 split: sources conflict.
- Audible's direct publisher onboarding terms (contract, minimum catalogue) are not public.

## 5. Economics: licence models for audio Bibles in apps; cost of human vs. AI production

### Takeaway
No publisher publishes a price list for audio-in-app licences; the visible models are free-for-ministry API access (Faith Comes By Hearing / Bible Brain) and per-monthly-active-user commercial licensing (API.Bible, text). Producing an 80-hour audio Bible costs roughly €9k–€20k with French narrators or $20k–$32k at US union-level rates before studio/editing, versus a subscription-level cost with AI voices — which Audible via ACX will not accept.

### Cited Findings
- Bible Brain (Faith Comes By Hearing / Hosanna): API use is governed by a developer licence agreement last modified 15 April 2021; keys are free to request. — [FCBH Bible Brain licence](https://www.faithcomesbyhearing.com/bible-brain/license); [faith.tools](https://faith.tools/app/bible-brain-api)
- A third-party review reports that the Bible Brain licence requires content to be given to end users free of charge and alignment with FCBH's statement of faith. — [learnofchrist.com review](https://learnofchrist.com/resources/bible-brain-api) (secondary; licence text not read)
- API.Bible (American Bible Society): commercial "Pro" plan from $29/month plus licence fees based on monthly active users and the translations chosen; public-domain and Creative Commons Bibles included at no extra cost; cached content must be refreshed every 30 days; some translations (e.g. NIV) are not available commercially. — [API.Bible express licensing](https://care.api.bible/article/409-express-licensing-for-commercial-use); [API.Bible licensing overview](https://care.api.bible/article/369-understanding-api-bible-licensing)
- Crossway: up to 500 ESV verses may be used in audio without a formal licence; more requires a request; Crossway is reported to license to organisations rather than individuals. — [Crossway permissions](https://www.crossway.org/permissions/); [Crossway audio form](https://crossway.org/permissions/audio)
- France narration: 150–250 € per finished hour, with 2–3 working hours per finished hour; one freelance profile quotes 110 € HT per finished hour. — [Superindep guide](https://creation.superindep.fr/guides-pratiques/metiers/voix-off-freelance/); [Malt profile](https://www.malt.fr/profile/sandrinecoutaud) (weak indicators, not a union scale)
- US narration: ACX requires at least $250 per finished hour for SAG-AFTRA pay-for-production deals ($100 PFH for royalty-share-plus). — [ACX help, SAG-AFTRA H&R](https://help.acx.com/s/article/acx-contributions-sag-aftra-health-retirement)
- SAG-AFTRA has more than 65 separate audiobook agreements, i.e. no single published minimum. — [SAG-AFTRA audiobooks](https://www.sagaftra.org/audiobooks)
- A 2026 blog gives $250–400 PFH on commercial publisher contracts. — [Voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/audiobook-narrator-compensation-statistics-2026/) (no primary source cited)
- ElevenLabs: free plan has no commercial licence and requires attribution; output generated on a paid plan may be used commercially and indefinitely, subject to service-specific terms and the prohibited-use policy; beta-service output may not be used commercially. — [ElevenLabs help](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/legal/can-i-publish-the-content-i-generate-on-the-platform); [ElevenLabs terms (EEA)](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use-eu)
- ElevenLabs itself cautions that many platforms, including Audible, may not accept AI narration. — [Publishers Lunch](https://lunch.publishersmarketplace.com/2025/02/elevenlabs-allows-authors-to-create-ai-audiobooks-for-free)

### Inferences
- Narrator-only budget for 80 finished hours: France 80 × 150–250 € = 12,000–20,000 € (8,800 € at the lowest quoted profile); US 80 × $250–400 = $20,000–32,000. Editing, proofing and mastering are extra unless the per-finished-hour rate is all-in; these totals are arithmetic on the cited rates.
- AI narration works for in-app streaming and for Spotify/Apple/Google-type channels with disclosure, but closes the ACX door; for an Audible ambition a human recording is the safe asset.
- In France a narrator contract must expressly assign performer rights per mode of exploitation (app streaming, download, third-party platforms, territory, duration), otherwise the developer does not own what it paid for **[unverified — background knowledge, lawyer]**.
- Bible Brain is the obvious low-cost source for licensed streaming, but its "free to end users" condition and ministry framing must be checked against any premium tier or advertising in the app, and it gives no right to resell on Audible.

### Gaps
- No published figures found for audio-Bible licence fees from Biblica, HarperCollins Christian (Zondervan/Thomas Nelson), Tyndale, Crossway, Société Biblique de Genève, Alliance biblique française or Litchfield/Scourby.
- No French union or collective scale for audiobook narration found (Les Voix rate sheets cover advertising).
- ElevenLabs current plan prices and any audiobook-specific clause in its service-specific terms not checked.
- Full Bible Brain licence clauses (caching, downloads, ads, attribution, termination) not read.

## 6. What a rights-request letter to a Bible publisher should contain

### Takeaway
Publisher forms converge on the same fields: who is asking (organisation rather than individual), exactly what content and how much, format, distribution channels, territory, and whether money changes hands.

### Cited Findings
- Crossway ESV audio form fields: name; whether audio only, print, digital; list of all distribution channels. — [Crossway audio permission form](https://crossway.org/permissions/audio)
- Crossway digital form fields: name, email, address, phone, individual or company, company name, whether sold as a product and retail price, additional formats, distribution channels. — [Crossway digital permission form](https://www.crossway.org/permissions/digital)
- Cambridge (KJV) form fields: number of verses, purpose, territory, format, estimated UK distribution. — [Cambridge Bibles Request Form](https://www.cambridge.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/Bibles%20Request%20Form%20-%20Permissions.pdf)
- Tyndale (NLT): requests go through a Permissions Questionnaire or permission@tyndale.com; more project detail speeds processing; permission is limited to the specific use and is not transferable; phoning delays the process. — [Tyndale permissions](https://tyndale.com/permissions)
- API.Bible's commercial licensing is priced on monthly active users and translations selected, showing that user counts are a pricing input. — [API.Bible express licensing](https://care.api.bible/article/409-express-licensing-for-commercial-use)

### Inferences
- A complete letter should cover: legal entity and contact (company/association, country, VAT/SIREN); the app (name, platforms, store links, languages, monthly active users and geographic split); exact recording requested (translation, edition, narrator, full Bible or portions); use (streaming only, offline download, background play, excerpts for sharing); technical protection (authenticated streaming, encrypted offline cache, no file export); territory (worldwide or FR/EU/francophone); duration and renewal; business model (free, ads or not, subscription, donations) and proposed consideration (flat annual fee, per-MAU, revenue share, or ministry gratis licence); attribution and copyright notice wording; reporting offered (plays/users per quarter); sub-licensing excluded; termination and takedown procedure; and, separately, any request for Audible/retail distribution, which is a different right.
- Ask the publisher to confirm it controls both the text right and the recording right (producer and narrator), since these can sit with different parties.

### Gaps
- Full field lists of the Tyndale questionnaire, Biblica and HarperCollins Christian forms were not retrieved.
- No French publisher form (Société Biblique de Genève, Alliance biblique française, Excelsis/BLF) was found.

## 7. French specifics: SCPP / SPPF / ADAMI / SPEDIDAM and blanket licences

### Takeaway
I found no blanket or statutory licence that would let an app stream audiobook recordings on demand in France; on-demand use needs the producer's individual authorisation, but this "no blanket licence for audiobooks" conclusion is an inference, not a statement found in a source.

### Cited Findings
- SPPF manages neighbouring rights of independent music labels; producers may manage their rights directly or join a collective management organisation. — [CB News](https://www.cbnews.fr/node/70072); [Artcena](https://www.artcena.fr/guide/droit-et-administration/droits-de-l-artiste-interprete/le-droit-voisin)
- The French statutory licence was extended to internet radio (non-interactive webcasting) for phonograms published for commercial purposes, and upheld by the Conseil constitutionnel; it concerns radio, not on-demand streaming. — [Concurrences](https://www.concurrences.com/fr/review/issues/no-4-2017/chroniques/secteur-public/licence-legale-le-conseil-constitutionnel-declare-conforme-a-la-constitution)
- A Ministry of Culture document recommends studying mandatory collective management of neighbouring rights for streaming — a proposal, not law in force. — [Ministère de la Culture](https://www.culture.gouv.fr/content/download/277702/3213855?version=3)

### Inferences
- On-demand streaming in an app is an exclusive-right use, outside the "rémunération équitable" regime collected for broadcast and public places; SCPP/SPPF repertoires are music-label catalogues and ADAMI/SPEDIDAM distribute performer remuneration rather than grant app licences. So the licence must come from the audio Bible's producer, who should warrant the narrator's rights **[lawyer]**.
- A self-produced recording avoids the question entirely, provided the narrator contract assigns the performer's rights.

### Gaps
- No source found that states explicitly whether an audiobook is a "phonogramme" under the CPI for SCPP/SPPF purposes, or that audiobook publishers are outside collective management for streaming.
- SOFIA/SCELF or SNE positions on audiobook streaming not researched.
