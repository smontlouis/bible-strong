The 1200 by 630 image a messaging app or a social network shows when a verse link is shared: the verse itself, its reference, its version and the mark.

It is a page of the site at card size, with the mark as its only ornament: its ring and its dot, enlarged, set in the top right corner, cut by the edge and pale, both in `brand` at 12% opacity. The reference opens the card in the sans family at 44px and weight 800, in `accent`, followed by the version code in a chip. The verse is the figure, in the serif family at 52px, in `ink`, in a block of 900px at most. It may run over the pale ring and dot, which stay well under it. The lockup closes the card in the bottom left corner, mark at 44px and name at 26px.

The card is always in the light theme, whatever the theme of the reader: a shared image has one version. Keep 64px clear above and below and 72px on the sides. A verse too long for its box steps down in size, to 38px at the least, and is then cut at a word with an ellipsis; it is never set smaller. The image is a flat PNG under 300 KB: no photograph, no gradient, no texture.

The cards for a Strong entry, a dictionary article, a commentary, a topic, a chapter and the site's default image keep this frame and change only what stands in the place of the verse.

The consumer provides the verse text, the reference as the page prints it (“Jean 3:16”), the version code and the language. These figures are proposals until the image generator exists; the generator needs every value written out, so it reads `tokens.json`, not the site's CSS.
