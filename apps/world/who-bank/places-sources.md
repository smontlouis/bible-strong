# Who am I? — places draft

Ten distinct places, five Old Testament and five New Testament contexts. Each
record has four bilingual clues ordered 4 → 3 → 2 → 1, from an incidental detail
to a well-known identifying event. The clues never state their own answer.

## Checks actually performed

All 40 anchor passages were fetched and read in Louis Segond through Bible
Gateway. The same 40 references were read in the public-domain KJV transcription
with explicit chapter and verse numbers:
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scrollmapper/bible_databases/master/formats/json/KJV.json

Eleven source links additionally span the adjacent verses needed to identify the
place, speaker or event (for example Mark 2:1–4 and Luke 24:13–18). Those extended
passages were also fetched and read. Each clue retains its own source link.
All clue text and explanations are paraphrases, not copied Bible verses.

Counts, distinct answers, category, bilingual fields and the 4/3/2/1 sequence were
validated. Old/New classifies the chosen narrative context, not an assertion that
the place appears in only one Testament.

## Editorial notes

- Emmaus uses sixty stadia/furlongs, explicitly tied to LSG and KJV; manuscript and
  translation traditions can differ on the distance.
- Sinai accepts Horeb as a conventional biblical alternate name; it does not claim
  a verified modern archaeological location.
- Babel accepts Babylon as the related city name. If strict naming of the Genesis
  episode is preferred, the reviewer can remove that alias.
- Difficulty progression and clue distinctiveness require human play-testing.
  A strong player can identify some places before the final clue by design.
