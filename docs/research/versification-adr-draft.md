# ADR-XXXX: Name every versification and convert verse keys through a published pivot

## Status

Proposed. Would amend the "no automatic conversion" clauses of
[ADR-0011](../adr/0011-model-the-clementine-canon-and-versification.md) and
[ADR-0012](../adr/0012-model-modern-catholic-bible-editions.md), and the part of
[ADR-0115](../adr/0115-read-the-verse-study-of-a-language-in-one-read.md) that serves one verse
study per language whatever the Bible.

## Context

Forty-three of the 47 Bibles declare the versification `bible-strong-default`, which names no
numbering:

- LSG, NBS, NVS78P, DBY and DBR match Paratext `org` (BHS / UBS GNT) in every chapter;
- KJV, KJF, BSB, ASV, NASB and the other KJV-like Bibles match Paratext `eng`, except in 3 John
  (14 verses) and Revelation 12 (17 verses);
- NEG79, OST and FMAR mix both numberings, and no public table describes them;
- LXX_FR numbers the Psalms as the Septuagint does.

Verse-keyed resources each follow one numbering:

- KJV: cross-references (Trésor), both Nave languages, about twenty commentaries, entities and the
  timeline;
- NRSV with Psalm titles as verse 0: BHG and its interlinear indexes;
- Hebrew: Rashi;
- LSG: French dictionary verse presences;
- the author's own numbering: dictionary source citations (Calmet cites the Psalms by the
  Vulgate).

Clients send the key of the verse they display unchanged. An LSG reader therefore sees the
cross-references, topics, commentaries and "word by word" Hebrew line of another verse. This
affects about 143 chapters and 3,150 verses, among them 62 Psalms. A KJF reader of a French verse
page sees the LSG words of another verse. Strong sidecars are safe: they reach BHG through token
ids, not verse numbers.

The verse sheet generated by the study assistant is cached and shared between readers. It needs
one key for "the same passage" across Bibles that number it differently.

Public tables:

- Paratext `.vrs` files (`eng`, `org`, `lxx`, `vul`, `rso`, `rsc`) are MIT, in
  `sillsdev/libpalaso` and `ubsicap/versification_json`. Each maps its versification to `org`.
- STEPBible TVTMS is CC BY 4.0. It offers per-chapter tests and verse-level actions.
- SWORD's French tables are GPL-2.0-only, which is incompatible with this GPL-3.0 repository.

## Decision

### Every Bible and every verse-keyed resource names a real versification

A versification id names one table. That table is a published Paratext base, plus an optional
Bible Strong overlay that lists only its differences, like a Paratext `custom.vrs`.
`bible-strong-default` is retired: each Bible declares the versification its coverage matches.

| Versification | Base and overlay | Bibles |
|---|---|---|
| `org` | `org` | LSG, NBS, NVS78P, DBY, DBR |
| `org` with a per-Bible overlay | `org` | S21, BDS, BHS, CHU, BFC, FRC97, NFC, PDV2017, TLV, DEL, SBLGNT, POV |
| `bs-eng` | `eng`, with Psalm titles belonging to verse 1 (no verse 0) | NLT |
| `bs-eng` with an overlay | as above, plus Revelation 12:17 / 13:1 or 2 Corinthians 13:13 | ESV, NIV, AMP; GW, CSB, NET |
| `bs-kjv` | `bs-eng`, plus 3 John 14 and Revelation 12:17 / 13:1 | KJV, KJF, BSB, ASV, DARBY, RLT, RWEBSTER, RV1895, NKJV, NASB2020, NASB1995, TR1624, TR1894; EASY with its own overlay |
| `bs-nrsv` | `eng`, with Psalm titles as verse 0, plus 2 Corinthians 13:13 | BHG and its interlinear indexes |

Every numbering built on `eng` first applies the shared overlay `eng-fixes`, which corrects
`eng.vrs` where our texts show it wrong or incomplete (Numbers 26:1, Isaiah 64:1, 1 Samuel 20:42,
1 Kings 22:43, 1 Chronicles 12:4).
| `bs-lau` | `bs-kjv`, plus Joel in four chapters and 3 John 15 | LAU (replaces `bible-strong-french-4-chapter-joel`) |
| `bs-neg79`, `bs-ost`, `bs-fmar` | `org` with large overlays | NEG79, OST, FMAR |
| `theotex-septuagint` | `lxx`, plus the ADR-0018 structure | LXX, LXX_FR |
| `clementine-vulgate` | `vul`, plus an overlay | VUL |
| `bible-strong-catholic-extended-esther-daniel` | `org`, plus Greek Esther and Daniel | BCC1923 |

The ids fixed by ADR-0011, 0012 and 0018 are kept and now name a table. Books 67 to 77 have not been
measured yet. Their overlays are written when they are.

In `bs-eng` and `bs-kjv`, a Psalm title belongs to verse 1. `org` 51:1-3 therefore maps to
`bs-kjv` 51:1, marked as partial. An LSG reader of Psalm 51:1 or 51:2 receives what the Trésor keeps
under `19-51-1`, including its references to the title, rather than a `19-51-0` key that nothing
publishes.

Every verse-keyed resource declares the versification of its keys in its publication manifest.
Where a resource does not follow one numbering throughout (dictionary source citations), the
declaration is made per work, and per book where needed.

Bible Strong tables use the Paratext `.vrs` grammar and map to `org`. They are written from our own
texts, from the verse-correspondence manifests of Resource Studio and from the TVTMS tests, and
never copied from SWORD.

### `org` is the pivot

Conversion between two versifications goes through `org`: `fromPivot(target, toPivot(source, ref))`.

New internal keys are written in `org`. That covers the verse sheet, its cache and the keys the
study assistant stores.

Existing resources keep their native keys. They are converted at read time, not re-keyed. Re-keying
a resource to `org` at publication is a later optimization, made only if read-time conversion is
measured to cost too much.

### Considered pivots

The pivot was chosen on 2026-11-27 between two options. Under both, resources keep their native
keys and are converted at read time, so a KJV-keyed resource read by an LSG reader needs an
LSG → KJV conversion whichever pivot is chosen. The pivot decides only the intermediate step of a
conversion and the versification of new keys.

**`org` (chosen).**

- It is the finest numbering where it matters most: a Psalm title keeps its own verses (LSG 51:1-2),
  apart from the verse that follows (51:3), in 62 Psalms. `org` is coarser in only a handful of
  places (Acts 19:40, 2 Corinthians 13:12-13).
- Every Paratext table maps to it directly, in one step, with no Bible Strong table in the middle.
- LSG, NBS, DBY, DBR, NVS78P and BHS are identical to it: for the default French Bible, a verse key
  converts to itself.
- Cost: re-keying the resources to the pivot at publication, if it is ever done, means republishing
  almost all of them (Trésor, Nave, about twenty commentaries, entities).

**`bs-kjv` (not chosen).**

- Most verse-keyed resources already use it, so a later re-keying to the pivot would touch only
  Rashi, the French dictionary verse presences and Calmet's citations.
- It folds a Psalm title into verse 1: LSG 51:1, 51:2 and 51:3 would all become one pivot key, and a
  verse sheet on the title could not be told apart from one on the verse after it.
- No public table describes it: every conversion would still go through `org`, then `eng`, then the
  Bible Strong overlay.

### One shared module

`@bible-strong/resource-domain/versification` holds:

- the `.vrs` sources with their licence notices;
- a compiled form of those sources;
- pure functions that load nothing, such as `toPivot` and `fromPivot`.

These functions return zero, one or several keys and mark a partial correspondence. The Resource
service, the study workspace, the public site and the study-assistant Worker all use the same
module.

### A verse without an equivalent gets nothing

Several resulting keys mean a merge or a split:

- the caller reads the union of those keys;
- it shows the resource's own reference, for example "v. 1 (KJV numbering)".

No resulting key means the resource has nothing for that verse. A neighbouring verse is never
shown in its place.

### Every published Bible is checked against its versification

A Bible fails publication when its coverage does not match its declared versification:

- the check compares the last verse number of each chapter, as `verseNumbersByBookChapter`
  publishes it;
- only declared exceptions are allowed (ADR-0010).

A Bible Strong table must also pass the Resource Studio verse-correspondence detector against the
text it describes.

### Clients convert before they read

The study workspace, the site and the Resource service convert keys in both directions:

- the reader's key into the resource's versification;
- the keys a resource returns into the reader's versification.

The keys a resource returns include cross-reference targets and Nave or entity verses.

The verse study and the BHG "word by word" line read the converted key.

Comparison is converted too (decided on 2026-11-27). This covers the "in other versions" block of
verse pages and of the app, version comparison, parallel reading and the assistant's passage widget.
They align verses through the table, not by numeric position, and each version keeps its own verse
numbers. A verse without an equivalent keeps its row with an empty cell opposite, for example the
two title verses of LSG Psalm 51 against the KJV. Merged or split verses share one row.

The verse study (ADR-0115) is still read once per verse and language, under the `org` key, with
two query parameters:

- `numbering=org`: the Resource API reads each part at its own verse (Trésor and Nave in `bs-kjv`,
  French dictionary presences in `org`…), and gives cross-reference targets and commentary verse
  ranges back in `org`;
- `reader=<versification>`: the numbering of the page's Bible. A resource whose numbering is not
  measured yet is read at the reader's verse, as before conversion.

Bibles that share the `org` key and the reader's numbering share the answer. Without `numbering`,
the route answers as it did, so the Worker can be deployed before the site.

### The verse sheet is keyed in `org`

The key is the language and the passage range converted to `org`. The range runs from the first to
the last converted verse, in `org` canonical order. Examples:

- LSG Malachi 3:19-24 and KJF Malachi 4:1-6 share one sheet;
- a Psalm title is left out of the key, whatever the numbering it is read in (decided on
  2026-11-27): LSG Psalm 51:1-6, LSG 51:3-6 and KJF 51:1-4 (whose verse 1 holds the title) all give
  `org` 51:3-6. A passage that is only a title keeps it. The rule is `passageKeyRange` in the
  shared module.

The language is part of the key, so sheets are shared only between Bibles of one language (LSG,
KJF, NEG79… in French).

The sheet stores its references as structured `org` references and renders them in the reader's
versification. It never writes a verse number into its text.

### User data is interpreted, not rewritten

Highlights, notes, bookmarks and relations keep their stored keys. When a record carries the version
it was made in (ADR-0011), its key is read in that version's versification.

Legacy records without a version are not converted: their key is read as it is, in the versification
of the Bible displayed (decided on 2026-11-27). Firestore is never rewritten.

## Consequences

- An LSG or KJF reader sees the cross-references, topics, commentaries, entities and original words
  of the verse they read. The assistant's "numbering may differ" notice becomes accurate.
- Each client pays a small lookup per read. The compiled tables only list exceptions, at a few
  hundred lines per versification.
- Four new Bible Strong tables must be written and validated (NEG79, OST, FMAR, LAU). The Bible
  annotée and Aquifer must be measured before they can be declared. Until then they are read
  unconverted, which is today's behaviour.
- The Nave and commentary contracts keep refusing verse 0. Neither `org` nor `bs-kjv` needs it. Only
  BHG (`bs-nrsv`) has it, and its keys are converted before they reach those contracts.
- The study workspace glossary entry **Verse key** ("independent of how that verse is displayed")
  gains a versification: a verse key is read in a named versification and converted through `org`.
- Embedding the Paratext files requires keeping their MIT notice. TVTMS, if used, is embedded as a
  derived table with attribution to Tyndale House and STEPBible.org and a record of the changes.
- Comparison by numeric position (ADR-0011) ends. Existing users see aligned parallel columns where
  they saw shifted ones.
